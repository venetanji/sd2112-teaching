# SD2112 · Week 13 lesson plan

**Poster fair and final quiz** · three-hour lecture-workshop (about 165 minutes of content plus a 15-minute break, with a buffer) · deck: `week13-classpoint.pptx` (33 slides, from the *Build PowerPoints* workflow artifact or a local build) · web: `venetanji.github.io/sd2112-teaching/week13/` (html deck with the fair clock and the thirteen-weeks sketch running live; `SD2112-week13.pdf` next to it) · **plus the quiz deck**, `week13-quiz.pptx`: thirty multiple-choice slides in ClassPoint Quiz Mode, kept on the classroom PC and on Blackboard, never in the repository · **plus the grading sheets**: one paper sheet per team per grader, the rubric's five criteria with a band and a line each, kept in the folder and on Blackboard, never in the repository.

## Purpose of this session

For us:

1. Run the **poster fair** (40 %) so that every team presents twice and visits twice: four rounds of twelve minutes, half A (teams 1–13) at the boards in rounds 1 and 3, half B (14–26) in rounds 2 and 4, with the clock on the screen, the swap called by the TAs, and five graders with one sheet per team.
2. Collect **peer feedback** that is usable at the grading meeting: one ClassPoint line per poster visited (*team number · one strength · one question*), names hidden, no student names in the text, exported after class and forwarded to each team; and a **peer prize** decided by the room (an image upload with the team number as the caption, the four most-uploaded as finalists, one vote each).
3. Make the instructor feedback legible before it arrives: what is read (poster, video, brief, log), the five criteria and their weights, the sheets, the grading meeting, the mark and a paragraph per team on Blackboard.
4. Close the course before the quiz: the thirteen weeks on the rules ↔ examples line (live), using vs incorporating, mediation, bias and data, the designer's turn, and the course question — *can a machine originate a design?* — answered by the room one last time and set against the arguments of the semester.
5. Run the **final quiz** (20 %) cleanly: thirty multiple-choice questions on weeks 1–12 and the playlist in ClassPoint Quiz Mode, a Blackboard test as the fallback, one attempt, proctored, the summary exported before the room empties.
6. Say goodbye properly: four sentences to keep, where the materials stay, how the course was made (its own process note, out loud), thanks to the TAs and the coordinator, one line for next year's students.

For students, by the end of the class they can:

- present their product at a board in three minutes by answering the five questions a visitor asks (the decision, the data, the bias, the guardrail, the relation), and ask the same five of another team;
- write one line of feedback that names a strength and a question a stranger's poster can act on, with no person's name in it;
- say how the forty percent is decided — four things read, five criteria, one meeting — and when and where the mark and the paragraph arrive;
- place any week of the course, and their own product, on the line from *write the rule* to *show the examples*, and give one sentence for each of the four recaps (using vs incorporating, mediation, bias and data, the designer's turn);
- answer the course question in one line with a *why* that uses the course's words, and recognise the three defensible positions (no, yes, it depends who chose);
- sit the final quiz in either lane without confusion: one question at a time, answer once, a closed question stays closed, Blackboard only when Gio says so.

## The activity, in detail: the fair, the prize, the quiz

Three things the room does today, and every one of them ends in a ClassPoint submission: the fair (one line per poster visited), the prize (an image and a vote), the quiz (in Quiz Mode, in the quiz deck).

**The fair (slides 12–13, 0:19–1:14).**

1. **Set-up, 1 minute (slide 12).** The short answer is opened on the classroom PC and stays open until the prize; the example line is read aloud: a team number, a strength that is one of the five questions done well, a question that is one of the five left open. Nicolò puts the html deck's slide 13 on the second screen with the team count set.
2. **Round 1, 12 minutes.** Half A (teams 1–13) at their boards, one presenter each, the video looping; half B walks with phones, three minutes a poster, four to six posters, one line per poster into ClassPoint. The five graders (Gio, Amber, WU Zhao, MA Jie, Nicolò when the clock does not need him) walk half A with the sheets: a band and a line per criterion, a note in the margin. The TAs keep visitors moving; a board with no visitor for a minute gets one.
3. **The swap, 2 minutes.** At zero the ring turns red and the bell shows; the TAs call the swap out loud; nobody moves before it. Half B goes to its boards, half A takes its phones out; Nicolò clicks for round 2.
4. **Round 2, 12 minutes.** Half B presents, half A visits, the graders take B's sheets. Same pace, same line per poster.
5. **Rounds 3 and 4, 12 minutes each, with swaps.** The second pass, said out loud at the start of round 3: visitors go to the posters they missed; a different member of the team talks at the board; the graders go back to the teams where the first sheet was thin, for a second opinion. The feedback wall on the screen is now long; Gio reads two lines aloud between rounds if there is a moment.
6. **The prize, 7 minutes (slides 14–15).** Everyone uploads one photograph of the poster they would give the prize to, caption = the team number, not their own team. Amber counts the captions as they arrive and writes the four most-uploaded numbers on slide 15; the room votes once; the winning team gets a participation star each, as for a challenge award, and thirty seconds at the goodbye to say what their model decides and what it may never decide. Then the break.

**The quiz (slide 27, 2:01–2:41).**

1. **Set-up, 3 minutes.** Slide 26's caption has just been read aloud. Laptops closed and away from the boards; phones on the desk with ClassPoint open; bags under the table. Nicolò switches to `week13-quiz.pptx` in the second window; the clock on slide 27 stays on the second screen, started with a click when question 1 opens. WU Zhao at the back, MA Jie at the side, Amber at the desk with the Blackboard test hidden and ready.
2. **Thirty questions, about 32 minutes.** Each question is a multiple-choice slide in Quiz Mode: one idea, four choices, one right answer, weeks 1–12 and the playlist in course order, revisiting the week-7 least-sure list. Nicolò opens the question, the room answers on their phones, Nicolò closes it at about a minute (earlier if everyone has answered) and moves on. A closed question stays closed: say so once at the start, not again. No talking, no second screen, no notes, no chatbot. Anyone caught with a second screen is noted by the TA and dealt with after class under the integrity policy, not in front of the room.
3. **Late arrivals.** Someone arriving after question 1 is seated at the front by a TA and joins at the question on screen; the closed questions count as unanswered. More than ten minutes late: the Blackboard lane on a laptop at the front, at the TAs' say, with the same 35-minute limit — the test is set to one attempt and submits itself.
4. **Failures.** A dead phone: the spare at the desk, or the Blackboard lane on a laptop. ClassPoint failing the room: Gio says "Blackboard", Amber makes the test visible, everyone switches together and the clock restarts at 35:00. Nobody switches alone; a student who is "on Blackboard" while the room is on ClassPoint has not taken the quiz.
5. **After the last question.** Phones face down until Nicolò closes it. Back to slide 28 for goodbye. Nicolò exports the Quiz Summary to Excel before leaving the room; Gio puts the marks on Blackboard with the group-project marks after the grading meeting.

TA roles: Nicolò drives both clocks and the quiz deck; Amber holds the team numbers, the sheets, the Blackboard test and the prize count; WU Zhao and MA Jie run the room, the swaps and the proctoring. All five grade during the rounds; Nicolò grades in the rounds where the clock is running on its own.
