## Purpose

Give each team a fair opportunity to explain the product and its evidence, give peers a useful way to engage with it, and close the course's conceptual arc. The syllabus quiz item remains a separate unresolved policy/logistics question. Operational reliability and equitable access matter more than adding another workshop or unannounced competition.

A visitor should be able to understand:

1. Who the product is for and what problem it addresses.
2. What the AI decides or generates within it.
3. What research informed the decision and where the evidence stops.
4. Which data, relationship, bias or consequence matters.
5. What the designer chose, what control remains with the person, and what remains uncertain.

## Fair protocol for review

- Teams present the current product concept and evidence honestly. Simulated behaviour is labelled; a polished mockup must not be described as an evaluated working model.
- Visitors ask about the decision, research, limitations and implications, not only visual polish.
- Use the existing rubric. Within the group-project rubric: research/context 30%, ethical/sociological impact 30%, communication/poster 20%, video 10%, collaboration/process 10%. These are subdivisions of the project, not additional course weights.
- Peer discussion is formative unless a separate approved assessment rule says otherwise. No peer prize, bonus mark or new peer-grading weight is introduced.
- Do not display private student information. Feedback should not solicit sensitive personal disclosures.
- If a display or video fails, follow the instructor's confirmed contingency; do not invent a penalty or allow an unapproved replacement submission.
