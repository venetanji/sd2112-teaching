## 05 / Two diamonds. Four kinds of work.

Four minutes. Official Design Council resource checked October 21, 2026: Discover understands rather than assumes the problem; Define reframes the challenge; Develop explores different responses; Deliver tests small-scale solutions, rejects and improves. ## 06 / AI can enter more than one phase.

Four minutes, completing framing. Primary final Cambridge open PDF read: methods pp.4-7, results/discussion pp.13-16 and Figure 5 p.16 visually inspected. 88 valid AI-user survey responses from 103 replies to 3,004 invitations; 10 selected professional designers in Turkiye in May-June 2024 think-aloud sessions using chosen tools on a lunchbox task. Low response, self-selection/experience/sector limits; coding primarily first author with second-author checking and no formal inter-rater reliability. These sample counts are context, not effect estimates. No randomized no-AI comparator, so do not infer superior outcomes or time savings. Application: AI can suggest an alternative question, but it cannot replace evidence from affected people. Distinguish use of AI in designing from AI incorporated in the product.

## 12 / Test the decision, not the decoration.

During selection. A satisfaction question may add context, but satisfaction alone is not proof of accuracy, safety or effectiveness. Existing code also requires an explicit task and comparator. ## 13 / Decide what would change your mind.

Final three minutes of Develop. Teams replace the example with their own ordinary low-risk task. Write a neutral prompt and a result that would challenge the design before looking at outcomes. The example comparison is formative, not a controlled experiment: order, familiarity and tiny convenience samples limit attribution. Do not manufacture a percentage pass target. Keep this contract beside the prototype.

## 16 / Three cards are enough.

Use within the build block, or print this slide as a three-card starter. Make the simulation visible to the participant BEFORE the task. The teammate follows a prepared branch, not a covert model impersonation. Invented cafe names and attributes are scenario props, not factual recommendations. Prepare one mismatch such as a suggestion outside the stated time constraint. Describe only its interface effects; do not imply measured hallucination rates.

## 17 / Can the person say no?

Last build checkpoint. Reconnect the established Week 5 mediation reading: a product helps shape what is noticed, interpreted and done. An override hidden after acceptance is not the same as meaningful choice before a decision. These are proposed design moves to test, not empirically validated remedies. Ask who must do the work of detecting a bad suggestion and whether the prototype makes that work possible.

## 18 / Give the task. Then watch.

115-145, one thirty-minute block across slides 18-20. Pair teams locally; no centralized student roster or new research recruitment. Consent is for a classroom walkthrough, not publication. Anyone may decline, pause or stop without pressure. No audio/video, personal health/legal/financial issues or sensitive data. Stop intrusive or unsafe tests; switch to an ordinary interface question. Internal walkthrough is an alternative with weaker outsider evidence, clearly labelled.

## 19 / Do not teach the answer.

Leave this on screen during walkthroughs if useful. The prompt permits rejection and does not demand a preferred answer. A think-aloud request may change behaviour; silence is also an observation. Do not interpret every pause as confusion without checking. After the attempt, ask what a label meant and what they expected. If two variants are compared, note order/familiarity; this small classroom exercise cannot isolate causal effects.

## 20 / Observation is not interpretation.

Use the final five minutes to compare observer and test-lead notes. Record behaviour as specifically as possible without identifiers. Satisfaction can be noted separately, never substituted for task outcome. A classmate is not automatically an intended user; a single successful task does not prove accessibility, long-term benefit, safety or model performance. A failure may be due to the task wording or the prototype, not only the participant. Keep notes local.
