## R1 / incorrect advice and explanation

- **Citation:** Cecil, J., Lermer, E., Hudecek, M. F. C., Sauer, J., & Gaube, S. (2024). Explainability does not mitigate the negative impact of incorrect AI advice in a personnel selection task. *Scientific Reports*, 14, 9736. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-60220-5 . Published 28 April 2024.
- **Version/access:** publisher open full primary article, not abstract-only. Methods, all five experiment methods/results, Discussion and Limitations read. No separate supplement, OSF data, analysis-script or preregistration audit. No correction notice encountered in the consulted article; this is not a comprehensive correction search.
- **Population/task:** N=1,403: Experiment 1a 370 students; 1b 242 HRM employees; 2a 328 students; 2b 143 students; 2c 320 students. German online fictional CV task; each participant reviews 16 two-page resumes, half eligible under strict criteria. No real employment consequences. Purported prescreening advice is manipulated, not an evaluation of a live recruiting AI.
- **Comparison/manipulation:** no-advice controls; correct/incorrect trials (generally 75% advice correct); human/AI source in 1a/b; AI with/without saliency heatmaps in 2a/b or criterion bar charts in 2c. 45-second review in 1a/b, 60 seconds in 2a, no limit in 2b; 2c uses the 2a design with changed visualization. Replications 1b/2b not separately preregistered although paper describes a preregistered series.
- **Measured outcomes/time:** suitability decision scored against criteria, perceived advice quality (usefulness/trust), decision confidence; immediate task outcomes, not longitudinal performance or hiring equity.
- **Support:** General procedure; Dependent variables; Experiments 1a-2c; Tables 2-7; Figures 3-9; Discussion and Limitations. Wrong-advice trials consistently lowered accuracy relative to no-advice baseline. Tested explanations did not significantly improve accuracy or reduce overreliance on incorrect advice; some rating/confidence effects differ by experiment. 2c confidence can increase without improved accuracy.
- **Important nuance:** overall advice/no-advice performance comparisons often nonsignificant; 2a explainable advice versus no advice improved overall performance. Do not turn wrong-trial harm into "all advice harms" or confuse that 2a comparison with an explanation advantage over non-explainable advice. A null is not equivalence; all modalities/settings were not tested.
- **Visible claim:** slide 11, "An explanation did not fix wrong advice here." Claim includes task/population, measured outcome and no-universal-explanations conclusion. Slide 31 gives the full evidence card.
- **Transfer/hypothesis:** slide 12's wrong-capacity room recommendation asks whether a reviewer can detect/refuse an explicit contradiction. It is labelled SIMULATION, low-stakes, not real personnel selection, replication or a validated interface remedy. Neither one successful rejection nor increased reported trust proves calibrated reliance.

## R2 / BLV visual privacy

- **Citation:** Sharma, T., Tseng, Y.-Y., Zhang, L., Ide, A., Mack, K. A., Findlater, L., Gurari, D., & Wang, Y. (2025). "Before, I Asked My Mom, Now I Ask ChatGPT": Visual Privacy Management with Generative AI for Blind and Low-Vision People. *ASSETS 2025*. Final publication record: https://doi.org/10.1145/3663547.3746335 .
- **Version/access:** full author preprint arXiv:2507.00286v2, 19 July 2025, https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00286v2 . Methods, findings sections 4-5, Discussion/Limitations and interview Appendix A read. Full primary author text, not final ACM typesetting; identity with final text not asserted. The author version retains unfilled conference-template metadata. Final typeset article/supplements were not independently compared.
- **Population/method:** 21 existing GenAI users in the US; 14 totally blind and seven with some light perception. One-hour remote Zoom interviews November 2024-January 2025, current practices plus six contextual scenarios; thematic coding. No intervention, randomized comparator, repeated efficacy outcome or prospective follow-up. Prose says 25-54 but Table 2 includes P12 18-24; do not teach an exact age range.
- **Support:** sections 3.1-3.3; 4.1-4.4; 5.1-5.6; 6.1-6.5. Participants described independence/privacy from helpers, uncertainty about provider processing/retention, shared-space and bystander data, and professional responsibility for others' documents. Views and practices vary; some prefer trusted human help for sensitive content.
- **Measured construct:** qualitative experiences, perceived limitations and future design preferences. Not prevalence among BLV people, current vendor-policy verification, accuracy, measured privacy gains, access effectiveness or tested redaction/retention control efficacy.
- **Visible claim:** slide 13, "Private from a helper. Exposed to whom?" Methods/limit remain on slide; full consulted-version link is visible. Slide 32 provides citation/version/access.
- **Transfer/hypothesis:** slide 14's capture/review/edit-or-refuse/share flow is our proposal, not tested protection. Remote analysis can already transmit data before final sharing; a later confirmation cannot undo provider disclosure. Nonvisual controls need participatory evaluation, not disability simulation. Paper/static fictional material suffices. Do not adopt reported risky practices as security advice or repeat speculative technical guarantees from section 6.4 as established facts.

## Theory, normative claims and archive boundaries

Slides 7-10, 14-17 and workshop revisions explicitly distinguish proposed design from empirical results. The accountability argument is normative course analysis, not legal advice, a measured causal law or an experimentally established universal remedy. Contestability requires a route and role with authority; adding a human label or disclaimer is insufficient evidence.

The Van Den Eede (2011) DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s10699-010-9190-y is retained as the existing syllabus background reading. Its full primary text was not newly retrieved. Slide 8 therefore calls its easy-use/visible-effects distinction **our working design distinction**, not a quotation or newly verified reading of that author. Existing Ihde/Verbeek mediation vocabulary is continuity, not a new full-book review. No ELIZA, current-product incident, legal ownership or historical Ihde-language factual claim is used; those would require separate primary verification.
