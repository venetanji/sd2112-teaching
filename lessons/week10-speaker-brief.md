**SD2112 · AI in Design · Friday 6 November 2026**

## For contributors

Help students see **how a real project moves through Discover, Define, Develop and Deliver — and what AI changes about that work**. Bring a concrete story, not a compressed thesis defence or four textbook definitions. Show decisions, alternatives, surprises and unfinished questions. Moving back to an earlier stage is part of the story, not a failure to follow the framework.

The working format is **a Double Diamond introduction, followed by four 10-minute speaker slots, each including Q&A (40 minutes total)**. The introduction will use one clear slide per stage. **Q&A is part of each 10-minute slot, not extra time afterward**; leave room for it when preparing your case. The introduction's duration and facilitator, speaking order and timekeeper still need agreement.

Your case should make those stages tangible. Distinguish **AI used to help design something** from **AI incorporated into the resulting product or service**; either can be discussed, but they are different claims. Connect to the preceding classes where useful: AI as design material and mediation; data, bias and privacy.

### Prepare for the Wednesday planning meeting

The meeting's exact date, time and joining details remain to be confirmed. Bring a reviewable outline or draft, not necessarily a polished talk:

- **One project and one takeaway:** what should students understand or do differently afterward?
- **The actual story:** who it was for, what you knew, what you assumed, a consequential design decision, and what changed it. Show where the project really reached; do not manufacture a completed four-stage journey.
- **A few shareable artifacts:** sketches, photos, prototype screens, alternatives, a short demo or observations you have permission to show. Mark anything private or permission-dependent; do not put it in this public repository.
- **The AI comparison:** what AI actually did, what you would now try differently with AI, and what still needs people, domain knowledge or real-world evidence. Clearly label retrospective ideas as proposals, not completed results.
- **One question for students:** something they can apply to their own project's current uncertainty.
- **Practical needs:** how you will divide your 10 minutes between the case and Q&A, equipment/access needs, offline fallback, and whether you can support the group activity. Speaking order and facilitation roles are not yet fixed.

**Nicolò:** use your own experience as the anchor, then explain how you would redo relevant parts with AI today. Compare a specific decision or artifact, rather than promising that AI would make everything faster or better.

**MA Jie:** use the Cantonese opera project to explain the relevant Double Diamond decisions and the role of AI. Let the actual project determine the story. Where relevant, discuss cultural context, whose expertise matters, representation, permissions and what an AI output cannot validate.

**Other contributors:** topics and contributions will be followed up separately. The four slots are not assigned to named contributors by this brief. You can use the same preparation structure with your own project.

A **provisional 5–7-slide case** is a useful starting point, not a required slide count. Fit the case and Q&A together within 10 minutes; use fewer slides if needed. Prefer fewer strong artifacts over dense slides. Bring questions and missing evidence to Wednesday; uncertainty is useful planning information.

### What students will do afterward

Teams may be at **any Double Diamond stage**. They will show where they are, ask another team for focused feedback, decide which stage or question needs attention next, and **act on that decision during class**. They need not all build or test a prototype. Some may revisit evidence or reframe the problem; others may compare alternatives or test an interaction.

Help them distinguish feedback from evidence: classmates are not automatically the intended users, generated personas are not user research, and a paper walkthrough cannot establish model accuracy, fairness or safety. The useful outcome is a reasoned next action and a visible change or justified decision to retain the current approach. No new graded submission or deadline is created here; existing project and Canvas requirements remain in force.

The working plan is a three-hour class with a protected break. The introduction and 40-minute speaker block should leave substantial time for the student activity rather than displace it. Giovanni will be absent; the lead facilitator and timekeeper still need agreement.

## Ready for review when…

- [ ] The story and outline are approved by the contributor; actual work, interpretation and AI counterfactuals are distinguishable.
- [ ] Students can understand the project and its consequential DD decisions without specialist background.
- [ ] Claims, visuals and permissions are accounted for; private or restricted material is excluded.
