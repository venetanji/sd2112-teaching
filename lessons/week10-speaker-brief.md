# Week 10 / proposed contributor brief

**Design a product with AI inside: Double Diamond decisions** / 30 October 2026

## Focus

How do we design an AI-enabled product or service through Discover, Define, Develop and Deliver, and what considerations belong in each phase?

This builds on **Week 8: AI as design material and mediation** and **Week 9: data, bias and privacy**. The focus is the AI incorporated in a product and its consequences for people, not using generative AI to make a designer's workflow faster. A concrete prototype or research case is useful; a compressed thesis defence is not required.

## Suggested common outline

1. **Need and context:** who is the product for, who else is affected, and what evidence supports the need?
2. **AI inside:** what does the model do, what do written product rules do, and what does a person decide? Why AI rather than a simpler approach?
3. **Design choices:** connect the case to the relevant Double Diamond phases. Show alternatives, iterations and returns to earlier framing where they actually happened.
4. **Concrete example or demo:** show an interaction, an AI output, a human choice and what happens when an output is poor or unsuitable.
5. **Evidence and consequences:** what observation, evaluation or failure changed the design? How did data, representation, harmful bias, privacy or mediation affect a decision?
6. **Limits and takeaway:** what remains untested, and what should students consider in their own AI-enabled products?

Cases may cover multiple phases. Do not manufacture a complete four-phase success story when the actual work is narrower or unresolved. Distinguish a simulated interface from measured model behaviour, and participant evidence from assumptions or generated personas. Describe only evaluation that actually happened; no guaranteed fairness, safety or bias-removal claims.

## Phase prompts, not mandatory slide sections

- **Discover:** actual need, affected/missing perspectives, relevant data and legitimate use.
- **Define:** reason for AI, model/rule/human boundary, useful outcomes and unacceptable harms.
- **Develop:** interaction alternatives and autonomy, uncertainty, correction/rejection, data minimisation and failure recovery.
- **Deliver:** interaction and model evaluation, distribution of outcomes, privacy-safe monitoring, responsibility and triggers for revision.

These are course application prompts, not an empirical checklist or an official Design Council AI prescription. Link Weeks 8-9 to specific design decisions, not a detached final ethics slide.

## Student application and practical boundaries

After the cases, teams map their own product across the four phases, prototype one consequential unresolved assumption, conduct a voluntary synthetic peer walkthrough, and revise one decision. A takeaway that helps this work is more useful than a survey of every technical detail.

Use shareable, non-sensitive examples. Do not expose student records, private participant data, credentials, real accounts or personal prompts. Label simulated demos, prepare an offline fallback and confirm equipment/access needs in advance. This brief adds no student assessment, upload or deadline.
