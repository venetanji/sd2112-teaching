## Learning goals

Students should be able to:

1. Explain how human-like cues shape perceived agency, expectations and relationships with AI products.
2. Distinguish response preference, perceived empathy, behavioural change and clinical outcomes.
3. Read a research claim with its study population, comparison, outcome, time horizon and limitations.
4. Find research relevant to their own product, rather than copying a lecturer's example.
5. Translate evidence into a scoped product decision and a testable question, preserving meaningful human control.

## Teaching spine

**Human-like cues → perceived care/trust → disclosure and reliance → possible benefit or harm → product responsibility.**

An AI can generate fluent, caring-seeming language without that establishing human understanding. The interaction may nevertheless have real effects on people. Ask what the product encourages a person to believe, do, disclose or delegate, and what evidence supports that claim.

Tool, adviser, teammate, companion and authority are examples to open discussion, not a fixed taxonomy. Students may identify other relationships. Include the less visible or apparently “magical” decisions in a product, not only products with a face or chatbot persona.

## Team activity worksheet prompts

Keep the detailed worksheet in ordinary team notes or the existing project workspace. The instructor-requested ClassPoint paper submission is individual: URL/DOI plus relevance, one different paper per teammate. No additional Canvas upload, grading rule or deadline.

1. Our product, intended user and context are… The AI decision and necessary data are… It will not collect, promise or do…
2. The AI relationship or attributed capability we need to investigate is…
3. Our search question, keywords and search route are…
4. For each paper: citation/link; relevant passage/page; method and comparator; finding; limitation; relevance to our product.
5. The strongest benefit evidence we found is… The strongest risk/problem evidence is… The evidence that complicates our initial view is…
6. Because of that evidence, we will keep/change/remove…
7. Our next test will compare… and observe… A result that would change our decision is…

Research is cumulative across lectures. Failure to find strong evidence should produce a narrower claim and a better research question, not a fabricated citation.
