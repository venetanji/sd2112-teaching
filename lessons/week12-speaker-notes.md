## 5 / "Find an available workshop slot."

## 7 / Persuasive is not justified.

This is the teaching distinction, not a universal psychological law. Week 11 called attention to mediation and curation; now ask whether the evidence and practical boundaries justify acting on a particular output in a particular setting. A description, explanation or warning is not by itself proof of accuracy. A visible control is not by itself a usable option. ## 9 / What was the system allowed to do?

NTSB HAR-20/01 pp. 12-15 describes the 2017 Tesla Model X Autopilot, a combination of traffic-aware cruise control and Autosteer. ## 11 / An invitation to intervene had a time limit.

NTSB HAR-20/01 pp. 5-6 reports the downloaded vehicle log: Autosteer initiated a left input about 5.9 seconds before collision; the vehicle entered the gore, lost its lead car about 3.9 seconds before impact and accelerated toward a preset cruise speed of 75 mph. Forward collision warning and automatic emergency braking did not activate. The speed at impact was about 71 mph. No braking or evasive driver steering was recorded. This timeline is a selected reconstruction of the final seconds, not a test of an alternative interface or a judgment of the driver.

## 12 / Could oversight actually happen in time?

The NTSB pp. 14-15, 20, 40 and conclusions distinguish a label or warning from operationally meaningful supervision. Wheel torque is an imperfect proxy for engagement: absence of measured torque does not prove where hands were at every moment. Earlier visual and audible alerts occurred, but none in the final 60 seconds; the report found the timing and engagement monitoring ineffective. The vehicle could be overridden, yet an override required recognizing and responding during a rapidly developing hazard. The NTSB also found the collision avoidance functions were not designed to detect this attenuator. The takeaway is not "driver at fault therefore design absolved" or its converse.

## 13 / An accident has more than one control point.

The NTSB probable-cause statement includes Autopilot steering into a gore due to system limitations and the driver lack of response associated with likely distraction and overreliance. The ineffective driver-engagement monitoring contributed to the crash. The unrepaired damaged attenuator contributed to injury severity; the report also discussed reporting and maintenance failures and federal oversight. These are not interchangeable assignments of legal fault. Do not blame the victim, erase the driver duty, or claim that software alone explains the injury outcome. Ask who could change each control point.

## 15 / Control before, during and after.

These three design alternatives are illustrative hypotheses, not retroactive proof that a specific feature would have prevented the 2018 crash. NTSB recommended operational-design safeguards and more effective driver monitoring, but safe fallback design must be tested for this context. Activation gating can wrongly exclude usable cases; more alerts can be missed; a false sense of security can persist. Recording data raises privacy and accountability questions. A product should articulate evidence for the particular permission it grants, and provide contestation and investigation when a failure occurs. This translates to recommender and booking systems too.

## 17 / Can people reject incorrect advice?

Cecil et al. (2024), published open primary study: five German online experiments, total N 1,403; immediate suitability decisions on 16 fictional CVs per person against strict criteria. No real hiring. Advice was approximately 75 percent correct; conditions included no advice, advice and explanations such as heatmaps or criterion charts. Figure 6a is only Experiment 2a: 328 participants overall, 214 advice recipients in the regression. Blue and pink bars group correct versus incorrect advice; orange and green bars group explanation versus no explanation across correct and incorrect trials. Four bars are two pooled contrasts, not four arms. Dashed no-advice baseline 70.61 percent; error bars are standard errors. Tested explanations did not clearly reduce reliance on wrong advice. A nonsignificant difference is not evidence that all explanations are equivalent. Read the axes first.
