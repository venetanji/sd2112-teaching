## 11 / An explanation did not fix wrong advice here.

Cecil, Lermer, Hudecek, Sauer and Gaube (2024), published full text, General procedure, Experiments 1a/1b/2a/2b/2c, Tables 2-7 and Discussion/Limitations read. Samples: 370, 242, 328, 143, 320 = 1,403. Each participant screened 16 fictional two-page CVs against strict criteria; half were eligible. Advice was purported human/AI prescreening, generally 75% correct, not a live deployed recruiter. No-advice controls; source manipulated in 1a/b and visual explanation in 2a-c. 2b removed time limits; 2c used criterion bar charts. Wrong-advice trials performed worse than baseline, not a universal overall harm from having any advice. Explanation effects on ratings/confidence varied; the tested explanations did not significantly improve task accuracy or reduce wrong-advice reliance. No consequences for actual applicants. Supplements/preregistrations not independently audited. About five minutes.
