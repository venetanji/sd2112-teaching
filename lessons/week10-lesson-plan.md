## Central question and continuity

**How do we design an AI-enabled product through the Double Diamond, and what must we consider at each phase?**

- **Week 8 / AI as design material and mediation:** distinguish using AI from incorporating it; examine what the product decides for a person and how it changes perception, action, judgment, control and responsibility.
- **Week 9 / data, bias and privacy:** examine the data behind that decision, representation and missing perspectives, harmful outcomes and research-backed ways to reduce them. Learned patterns are not automatically harmful bias; identify the disadvantage and mechanism in context.
- **Week 10 / application:** carry those questions through Discover, Define, Develop and Deliver. They recur throughout; testing can reopen the original framing.

## Phase-by-phase design considerations

These AI-product prompts are the course's teaching synthesis of the Design Council framework and Weeks 8-9, not an empirical result or an official Design Council AI checklist.

| Phase | Product with AI inside: questions and decisions | Evidence or next check |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | Whose need and context? How would the product change experience, behaviour and relationships? Who is affected or missing? What data exists, who does it represent, and may it be used for this purpose? | Relevant user/context evidence, source passages, representation and permission gaps. Generated personas are not evidence from affected people. |
| Define | What problem are we solving, and why does AI belong? Separate model output, written product rules and human choice. Specify useful outcomes, unacceptable harms and data limits. | Justify the AI role against a simpler baseline; identify the assumption whose failure would change the design. |
| Develop | Explore different interactions and autonomy levels. Design understandable uncertainty, correction, rejection, data minimisation and recovery from a poor output. Consider biased results and who does the extra work. | Compare alternatives and prototype a consequential decision, including a poor-output path. Treat mitigations as hypotheses, not guaranteed safeguards. |
| Deliver | Evaluate the interaction and actual AI behaviour separately. Examine useful outcomes and failures across relevant contexts. Plan feedback, recovery, responsibility and privacy-safe monitoring. | Distinguish a classroom walkthrough from model evaluation and deployment evidence. Define what would trigger revision, rollback or a return to Discover/Define. |

This stays in the existing project workspace; it is not a new graded submission, compulsory poster section or upload.

## Workshop protocol and substeps

### Map / 15 minutes

0-4: need, affected people, data and missing evidence. 4-8: AI role, human choice, useful outcomes and harms. 8-12: alternative interactions, failure paths and evaluation needs. 12-15: select one consequential uncertainty. For each phase retain a decision, its evidence or assumption, a risk and the next check. This maps ongoing work; it does not claim to complete four research phases in fifteen minutes.

### Develop and prototype / 30 minutes

0-5: compare two responses and choose the uncertain decision. 5-10: write a neutral task, observable outcome and what would change your mind. 10-25: build request, AI output, choice and recovery. 25-30: label simulation and rehearse. Paper, role-play, Figma or existing code is fine. A non-AI baseline can help justify the AI role or offer recovery; it does not remove the AI-inside project focus.

The fictional cafe example uses three cards: request, simulated suggestion, choice/recovery. It needs no actual location, account, purchase or model call. A teammate reveals prepared branches, visibly labelled as simulated AI. The cafe need and outputs are invented teaching props, not research findings. Paper can test interpretation and interaction, not actual ranking accuracy, model reliability, fairness or safety.

### Voluntary walkthrough / 25 minutes

0-3: explain simulation, voluntary participation and right to stop. 3-11: first walkthrough. 11-19: swap if willing. 19-25: compare notes. Declining is fine: use an internal walkthrough and label its weaker outsider evidence. Give the task without coaching a preferred answer; note any help. Separate observed behaviour from tentative interpretation. A classmate is not automatically an intended user.

Synthetic scenarios only; no recording, names, sensitive stories, transactions or recruitment. Keep notes private. This is formative classroom learning, not publication consent, controlled causal evaluation or clinical/legal/high-stakes validation.

### Revise and share / 10 minutes

0-5: revise one consequential decision and retain before/after. 5-8: exchange evidence and limits with neighbours. 8-10: record the next check and who should act. A defensibly unchanged decision is acceptable. Use: "We changed ___ because ___. This does not establish ___." Update the phase map, including an earlier framing if necessary. Name the actual model, affected-user or longer-term evidence still needed; do not invent validation or begin unauthorized collection/deployment.
