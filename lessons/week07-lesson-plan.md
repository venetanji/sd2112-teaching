# SD2112 · Week 7 lesson plan

**Mid-term: quiz, pitches, teams** · three-hour lecture-workshop (about 165 minutes of content plus a 15-minute break, with a buffer for the pitches) · deck: `week07-classpoint.pptx` (34 slides, from the *Build PowerPoints* workflow artifact or a local build) · web: `venetanji.github.io/sd2112-teaching/week07/` (html deck with the two p5.js sketches and the quiz clock running live; `SD2112-week07.pdf` next to it) · **plus the classroom copy**, `week07-quiz.pptx`: this deck with the twenty quiz slides appended as slides 35–54 in ClassPoint Quiz Mode, kept on the classroom PC only, never in the repository (the same twenty questions are in the Blackboard test).

## Purpose of this session

For us:

1. Run the **mid-term quiz** (10 %) cleanly: twenty multiple-choice questions on weeks 1–6 and the playlist, in ClassPoint Quiz Mode, with a Blackboard test as the fallback, one attempt, proctored, and the marks on Blackboard within the week.
2. Close module 2: the Challenge 5 vote, the **individual reflection** (20 %) submitted today with its process note, and the six weeks recapped in one picture (the rules ↔ examples line) before the quiz.
3. Open the **group project** (40 %) properly: the sentence (*a model decides something for each person, and you account for what that does to them*), the three deliverables, the one-page mediation brief and its four questions, the rubric, and the six milestones.
4. Get every idea in the room said out loud in **sixty seconds**, in one format (the person, the decision, for whom, with what data), and every student into a **team of four or five** with mixed skills and a scribe, registered on ClassPoint and on Blackboard before they leave.
5. Keep the door open for a guest lecture without breaking the timing: a placeholder slide, a pitch cap, and a run of show that works with or without it.

For students, by the end of the class they can:

- place a tool or a product on the line from *write the rule* to *show the examples* and say which four words apply (exact / fluent, explainable / opaque, brittle / typical, hand-written / learned);
- say what the group project asks for in one sentence, and test any idea against it (*does a model decide something for each person?*);
- name the three deliverables, the four questions of the mediation brief, the five criteria of the rubric and their weights, and the six milestones;
- pitch an idea in sixty seconds in the four-beat format, and write three words about someone else's;
- say who is in their team, what kind of work each person brings, who the scribe is, and what is due in week 8.

## The activity, in detail: the quiz, the pitches, the teams

Three things the room does today, and every one of them ends in a ClassPoint submission: the quiz (in Quiz Mode, on the quiz slides of the classroom copy), the pitches (three words per idea, from everyone), the teams (one line per team, from the scribe).

**The quiz (slide 13, 0:27–0:55).**

1. **Set-up, 3 minutes.** Slide 12's third card has just been read aloud. Laptops closed; phones on the desk with ClassPoint open; bags under the table. Nicolò types `35` and Enter: the first quiz slide, in the same slideshow, so nobody rejoins ClassPoint; the clock on slide 13 stays on the second screen, started with a click when question 1 opens. WU Zhao at the back, MA Jie at the side, Amber at the desk with the Blackboard test hidden and ready.
2. **Twenty questions, about 22 minutes.** Each question is a multiple-choice slide in Quiz Mode: one idea, four choices, one right answer, weeks 1–6 and the playlist in course order. Nicolò opens the question, the room answers on their phones, Nicolò closes it at about a minute (earlier if everyone has answered) and moves on. A closed question stays closed: say so once at the start, not again. No talking, no second screen, no notes, no chatbot. Anyone caught with a second screen is noted by the TA and dealt with after class under the integrity policy, not in front of the room.
3. **Late arrivals.** Someone arriving after question 1 is seated at the front by a TA and joins at the question on screen; the closed questions count as unanswered, however late they are. There is no individual Blackboard lane during the class: the test is hidden until Gio says so, and a student who misses the quiz for a documented reason goes to Gio afterwards, under the programme's rules on missed assessments.
4. **Failures.** A dead phone: the spare phone at the desk, or classpoint.app in a laptop browser at the front, on a TA's say. ClassPoint failing the room (the join drops, the question does not appear on the phones): Gio says "Blackboard", Amber makes the test visible, everyone switches together and the clock restarts at 25:00. Nobody switches alone; a student who is "on Blackboard" while the room is on ClassPoint has not taken the quiz.
5. **After the last question.** Phones face down until Nicolò closes it. `14` and Enter: back to slide 14 for the least-sure question, then the break. Nicolò exports the Quiz Summary to Excel before leaving the room; Gio puts the marks on Blackboard within the week.

**The pitches (slides 23–27, 1:39–2:21).**

1. **The format (slide 24), 4 minutes.** Read the bar with the example: ten seconds for the person, twenty for the one thing the model decides, ten for who else and who is left out, twenty for the data and where it comes from. No slides, no demo, the product's name last. The test the room applies to every pitch is slide 18's: does a model decide something for each person?
2. **The pitches (slide 25), up to 34 minutes.** The sign-up list from the door sets the order; WU Zhao calls the next name while the current pitch runs. Nicolò clicks the timer at the first word; the ring drains, orange at fifteen seconds, red at zero — at zero the pitcher stops and the next one is already walking up. The join activity (slide 26) is open on the second screen throughout: everyone writes three words for every idea they would join, as many times as they like. Thank, next. Stop at thirty pitches or at 2:21.
3. **The wall (slide 26), 2 minutes.** Put the join wall up and read the three most-named ideas; ideas with no lines will need to merge at the forming round — say so kindly. Names are hidden (the wall would show ID codes at best): the wall counts demand, and the walk to the numbered sheets does the matching.
4. **The domains (slide 27), 2 minutes.** One word each: the cloud shows where the room is going and where nobody is. Point at both.

**The teams (slides 28–31, 2:21–2:41).**

1. **What a team needs (slide 29), 3 minutes.** Five kinds of work, not five roles: research, concept and prototype, visual, video and story, the scribe. Everyone does two; every deliverable needs a line into it. The scribe keeps the log — the ten percent most teams lose.
2. **Forming (slide 30), 12 minutes.** The pitchers stand at the numbered sheets along the walls, each holding their three words. Everyone else walks to an idea. A spot is full at five; the sixth person moves on. Four is a team, three is not yet. Nicolò calls six, nine and twelve. At nine minutes the TAs start placing whoever is still walking; a table of six sends two people to the nearest table of three; ideas nobody named merge or come to the desk. At twelve, nobody is without a team — the TAs place the leftovers, today, not by email later.
3. **Register (slide 31), 4 minutes.** Scribes only: the team name and the idea in one line, no people's names in the line. Put the wall up and read five: every line should have a person, a decision and a source of data in it; where one is missing, say so — that is the proposal's first correction. Amber opens the Blackboard group sign-up; the scribe adds the members before the room empties; the TAs check the numbers against the wall.
4. **Before week 8 (slide 33).** The team on Blackboard before leaving the room (by tomorrow Amber registers the rest from the scribes' lines); the proposal, one page (the four beats, written down), drafted before the class and finished in it; one product that decided something for you today, and a line on what it decided.

TA roles: Nicolò drives the deck, the jump to the quiz slides and every timer; Amber holds the reflection deadline and the Blackboard test, and opens the group sign-up; WU Zhao and MA Jie proctor, run the sign-up list and the numbered sheets, and place the leftovers.
