## 15. Trace four different decisions.

40-62 minutes / Agency and authorship. Generation, selection, revision and authorization are different forms of action. A machine proposing a draft does not remove the upstream creator or the person who approves release. The diagram is an instructor contribution map, not a legal authorship determination. Use the fictional guide: the team chooses a sourced account and records why it rejected an unsourced fluent one.

## 16. Feeling, work, permission, law.

40-62 minutes / Agency and authorship. Four questions: felt ownership, actual credited contribution, permission to use another person's work, and copyright protection for human expression. The last two do not follow from how hard someone worked or how much they liked the output. US Copyright Office Part 2 (January 2025) addresses US copyrightability, not Hong Kong rights or licenses to train. Joshi and Vogel measure feelings in short prompting tasks; the study does not establish legal rights. Explain each without an automatic yes/no answer.

## 25. A complaint is not a verdict.

These are plaintiffs' allegations, not findings. Suno's answer, Document 28 filed August 1 2024, located by docket-indexed primary filing but host PDF returned 403; fair-use position corroborated by Reuters on August 1 2024: https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/music-ai-startups-suno-udio-slam-record-label-lawsuits-court-filings-2024-08-01/ . Warner Music Group's November 25 2025 primary announcement says its litigation with Suno settled and new licensed models were planned. It does not establish that every Suno model or all historical training was licensed. First ask who supplied recordings, who decided to train, who could grant permission and who might receive credit. Do not treat a US lawsuit as Hong Kong law. Last directly read primary records: complaint and Warner release on November 12 2026; response identified but full filing not retrieved.

## 31. Use one question to guide your change.

105-115 minutes / One activity: instructions and example. Read only the option the group chooses. Mediation concerns how the AI changes what a person notices or understands. Curation concerns what the team selects and leaves out. Agency/authorship concerns who decides and contributes. Responsibility concerns who responds when something goes wrong. More than one may apply, but choose one focus for this exercise. Do not require four analyses.

## 32. One topic. One problem. One change.

105-115 minutes / One activity: instructions and example. Model the same task students will do. We choose curation and power, not all four topics. The problem is that the guide only has museum sources. The proposed change is to ask a fishing family for an account and permission, then provide a clear story choice. Until there is an actual interview and permission, label the missing story rather than simulate a family voice. This proposed interface has not been tested; ask how the team would find out whether it helps.

## 33. Make your change.

115-145 minutes / Apply one topic to the project. Thirty minutes of actual group work. TAs ask which topic the group chose, where it appears in the project, who is affected and what they will change. If a prototype does not exist, annotate a concept sketch. Separate observed problems from assumptions; a proposed change is not a tested success. Keep work in existing project notes or drafts. No extra upload, new grading criterion or required paper search.

## 34. Explain it. Hear a question. Improve it.

145-165 minutes / Explain and improve the same change. This is the feedback stage of the same activity, not a second audit. Pair with one other group; do not impose a four-group quota. Each team gives a sixty-second explanation, then asks one clarifying question. Reserve the last ten minutes for revision. A team can reject advice with a reason. If pairing is impractical, use a TA or discuss within the group. Nothing new is collected or graded.

## 35. Show the change, not a checklist.

Compare how the topic changed a concrete design decision, not which group named the most ethical concepts. Keep assumptions visible, avoid exposing private records and do not imply that a peer-approved sketch has been tested. If projection is unavailable, share verbally.
