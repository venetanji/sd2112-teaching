## 03 / What relationship does your product invite?

Two-minute pair discussion within the ten-minute opening. Ask for one product feature and the expectation it invites, not a personal story. The list is deliberately open, not a classification test. A recommender with no face can still feel authoritative. Transition: the relation is not simply a property of the object.

## 04 / Technology shapes the relation.

Recall Week 5 without repeating its history of philosophy. Human-technology-world names a situated relation, not a one-way data pipeline. Embodiment: look through a viewfinder. Hermeneutic: read an image or display as a representation. Alterity: face the technology as a quasi-other. Background: systems shape the setting without focal attention. One product can participate in several relations. Source: Ihde (1990), Technology and the Lifeworld; Verbeek (2015), Interactions 22(3), 26-31; established course diagram.

## 05 / An apparent other is still a design relation.

A ticket machine can answer back in an alterity relation; a human face is not required. Conversational AI intensifies possibilities through language, voice and apparent continuity. Do not equate fluent response with consciousness or dismiss real effects because a system is not a human. The world recedes in the schema, not disappears. Historical 2025 slides used additional transparent/opaque alterity notation; do not present that extension as Ihde's original four-category scheme.

## 06 / A cue can become a promise.

Instructor-authored examples, not quotes from a product or empirical causal estimates. Ask which cue implies memory, availability, confidentiality or competence. A warm tone can be useful without pretending to be human. The arrows are questions to investigate: studies below test particular pieces, not this whole universal chain. Ask what happens when the expectation outlasts the actual capability.

## 07 / Effortless for whom?

Three-minute design analysis, not a claim about all products. Reconnect Machine B generation and the Week 4 agent harness: model output, retrieval, memory and interface policies are separate choices. Reading a generated explanation does not reveal a faithful internal reasoning trace. Ask teams to locate one hidden decision in their own concept.

## 08 / Can the person disagree and act?

Finish mediation by asking what the interface makes possible, not whether it generates a reassuring disclaimer. Trust should be calibrated to the task and actual reliability; maximum trust is not the objective. These are design questions, not experimentally proven universal safeguards. Transition to testing the evidence behind product promises.

## 09 / A paper is a bounded claim.

Use this frame aloud on each following study. Add the limit: what alternative explanation remains, and who is not represented? A correlation alone cannot establish causation. Randomization can support a causal inference within the tested conditions, not every population or interface. A working paper is not equivalent to peer-reviewed evidence. Ask students to name the outcome before debating whether the result is good or bad.

## 10 / A good-rated reply is not treatment.

Hatch et al. (2025), full PLOS article and correction reviewed. Methods: 830 US panelists, selected responses from 13 convenience-sampled experts and prompted ChatGPT-4; AI responses were longer. Common factors include caring/understanding, not patient symptom change. Eighteen hypothetical snapshots, no treatment delivered. August correction DOI 10.1371/journal.pmen.0000426 discloses internal BYU Psychology funding to SRB and lead author SGH's ownership/compensated therapy practice at Hatch Data and Mental Health; business did not fund the study and no clients were included. Original funding/COI statements are superseded; correction does not revise outcomes. Product inference: a convincing conversational surface does not establish the outcome a user needs. Ask for the missing outcome and comparator. About three minutes.

## 11 / Source awareness and authenticity ratings.

Jain, Pareek and Carlbring (2024), full PMC article reviewed. Initial 140 respondents believed all replies were human; 111 returned six months later. Human writers were Master's clinical-psychology students, not the R1 expert pool. Sections 2.1-2.5 and Tables 1/3: human ratings rose; AI ratings changed little. Table 1 supports authenticity superiority only; a Discussion sentence overstates professionalism/practicality and conflicts with the table. Nonsignificance is not equivalence. Anonymous responses prevented individual paired analyses. Time, attrition and familiarity are alternatives to a pure label effect. Perception, not treatment. Ask whether hiding the source would be justified: these findings do not justify concealment.

## 12 / A preferred reply is not a chosen source.

Wenger, Cameron and Inzlicht (2026), full published article reviewed. Four brief nonclinical studies, 691 source-choice participants. Explain evaluating a response versus selecting an empathy source, not an established relationship or repeated live dialogue. Sources were CHOSEN, not randomly assigned for the rating comparison: selection can affect experienced-response ratings. Mostly decontextualized empathizers/human strangers; transfer to ongoing relationships is untested. Do not infer that everyone rejects AI or that AI understands more because a response rates well. Ask whether source choice is part of a rehearsal product's value. No personal disclosures.

## 13 / Relief now. A step towards someone.

Allow five minutes for both, rather than two extra slides. R6: De Freitas et al. first online June 2025, issue April 2026. Distinguish within-session relief from sustained between-session improvement; feeling heard is a studied mechanism, not proof of reciprocal care. R7: Maheshka, Folk and Dunn, 2026; follow-up conversation initiation is self-reported. The control is an unrelated chatbot interaction, not human rehearsal; no claim of superiority to human preparation or improved conversation quality. Product inference, not tested here: an AI product could scaffold real-world contact instead of maximizing time in the chat. Ask what outcome would distinguish support from substitution.

## 14 / What would outcome evidence need?

Optional two-minute contrast; shorten detail, not team research. Heinz et al. (2025), full published article reviewed via public mirror because publisher endpoint was blocked; supplement and separate disclosures not obtained. Methods pp. 3-4: adults with clinically significant depression/anxiety or high-risk eating-disorder concerns; active suicidality, mania and psychosis excluded. Expert-built system, all responses supervised POST-transmission by trained clinicians/researchers, with correction and safety intervention. Tables pp. 5-6 support symptom findings. Access continued during weeks 4-8, so eight-week improvement is not maintenance after withdrawal. Waitlist is not an active human-therapy comparator; alliance comparison uses historical norms, not randomized parity. Do not transfer to unsupervised general companions.

## 15 / Agreement is not the same as help.

Sharma et al., Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models, ICLR 2024, arXiv 2310.13548. Connect behaviour to training/evaluation and product feedback, not an intention to flatter. Instructor-authored contrast, not study stimuli: supportive response acknowledges a feeling while questioning an unsupported conclusion; sycophantic response endorses the conclusion because the user holds it. Do not imply that every current model always agrees.

## 16 / Validation can change the next choice.

Cheng et al. (2026), final Science eaec8352 full article via public mirror; publisher blocked, supplement not reviewed. Final experiments: 804 + 801 vignette participants and 800 in an eight-turn live interpersonal-conflict chat; total 2,405, not preprint 1,604. Methods/results PDF pp. 5-7. Binary affirming versus disapproving responses, no neutral user-study baseline. Immediate repair/rightness contrasts are experimental; measured traits or benchmark associations are not causal for that reason. Prosocial intentions are not observed reconciliation; trust/return ratings are not diagnosed addiction. US/English context, no repeated-use longitudinal harm demonstration. Product inference: evaluate downstream choices as well as liking. Ask whether a kind but accurate challenge could differ from these binary conditions.

## 17 / When assistance becomes surrender.

Shaw and Nave (2026), Thinking-Fast, Slow, and Artificial. Full 58-page January 11 PsyArXiv v1 read; related SSRN February revision not retrieved or verified byte-identical to supplied PDF. Working paper, not peer reviewed. Procedures pp. 18-22: seven adapted Cognitive Reflection Test items, optional GPT-4o with seeded correct or confidently faulty replies; person retains final answer. Synthesis N=1,372 includes an ancillary sample, not Study 1 alone. The 79.8% faulty-answer adoption is conditional on AI-consulted trials, NOT 80% of all users. Incentives PLUS feedback reduced, not eliminated, faulty adoption; do not isolate their effects. Limitations pp. 48-49: narrow controlled snapshot, no longitudinal deskilling. Proposed System 3 is a theoretical account, not anatomy. Candidate design test: independent estimate before suggestion or easier comparison, not an established fix.

## 18 / An update can change the relationship.

Use the verified official account, not a reconstructed screenshot or unsourced incident detail. Separate vendor explanation from independent research. Do not include the optional BBC case: the full report has not been verified here and a non-graphic anecdote still cannot establish causation. Friend is not necessary for this teaching decision; omit unverified current product claims rather than inventing them. Ask how a version change could invalidate an earlier product test.

## 19 / Data. AI. Experience.

Work through one example: collect a fictional scenario, not a real identifiable transcript; generate alternatives, not a judgment about who is right; let a person edit, reject and leave. Data: minimum needed, access, retention/deletion. AI: what feedback rewards, source uncertainty and failure modes. Experience: what warmth or memory implies, what the user actually does next. These are candidate design responses, not proven safeguards. Psychological ownership is not legal ownership: optional R11 stays in appendix. R10 can deepen privacy later.

Teams need project-specific papers; do not require every project to become a mental-health product. End lecture by asking which assumption about their AI + X is least supported.

## 27 / Is this evidence about your product?

Near the end of research, ask each team for one match and one mismatch. A loneliness study does not directly validate a fashion recommender; a student sample is not every older adult; a supervised intervention is not an unsupervised consumer app. No relevant full text found? Narrow the claim, state access limits and make the next search explicit. Absence of retrieved evidence is not proof of no effect. Transition only after the forty-minute block is protected.

A future user study may require ethics approval. Ask about a competing explanation and what the small test cannot show.
