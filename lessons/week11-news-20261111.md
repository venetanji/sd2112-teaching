## Adobe's creative agents: an announcement to examine

Adobe's 10 November announcements describe
[agents in Creative Cloud tools](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/11/adobe-transforms-creative-cloud-tools)
and an [expanded Firefly offering](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/11/adobe-firefly-expands).
The creative application agents are beta offerings. Local MCP connectivity is
a preview, with private beta planned for early 2027, not a generally available
classroom feature. Product capability and productivity descriptions are
Adobe's claims; these announcements are not independent evidence of improved
design quality, accessibility, safety or legal permission.

**Course connection:** more steps performed by a tool make the selection trail
more important. Ask who supplied the inputs, proposed alternatives, rejected an
output and made the final choice. Separate credited contribution, permission
to reuse material, psychological ownership and legal copyright. Neither a
product label nor a recorded action establishes all four.

## Optional three-minute framing example

Use this within the existing 0-15-minute "Recover the decision" discussion.
Replace a short opening example rather than extend the lesson.

Show three original paper sketches for a fictional poster, labelled A, B and C.
No Adobe access or generation is required. Imagine that a creative agent proposes
B and revises its crop; this is an invented teaching scenario, not an observed
product test.

- Which stated criterion would justify choosing B? What did A and C offer
  that the selection removes?
- What record would let a teammate distinguish an agent's proposal from the
  designer's acceptance and later revision?
- Who could contest an inaccurate or exclusionary choice, and where would
  correction or reversal be possible?
- What source or permission would be needed before including a borrowed image?

Carry one answer into the existing evidence-and-curation audit. Teams can use
their current concept or notes; a completed prototype or polished poster is
not assumed. This adds no new hand-in or software requirement.

## Secondary comparison, only if useful

Kiro's 5 November [Crew bots announcement](https://kiro.dev/blog/crew-bots/)
and [documentation](https://kiro.dev/docs/crew/bots/how-bots-work/) describe
persistent bots, gateway-based scheduling and credit consumption. Trust mode
is the default and a workspace is not a filesystem security boundary. Ask
what a user would need to see and control before allowing repeated actions
on their behalf. Do not install a bot, grant access or test with student work.
